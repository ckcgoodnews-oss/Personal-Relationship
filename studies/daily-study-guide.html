Guides Within the Guide

1. How to Create a Good Study Question

Start with a real interest, concern, quality, decision, Bible statement, meeting point, or question. Turn a broad topic into a focused question that can guide a short study.

Too broadBetter question
PrayerWhat can I learn from Bible prayers that could improve my own prayers?
TrustWhat does trusting Jehovah require when I have a difficult decision?
ForgivenessWhat can help me forgive when I still feel hurt?
EnduranceWhat helps a Christian keep going when a trial lasts a long time?

Think about: Proverbs 18:15; Matthew 7:7, 8.

2. Observation Guide — Before Researching

Repetition

What word or idea keeps appearing?

Contrast

What two courses, attitudes, or outcomes are compared?

Cause / Effect

What causes what? Watch for because, for, so, therefore, if, then.

Command

What am I told to do?

Warning

What danger is identified?

Promise / Blessing

What good result is described?

Choice

What did a person do and what happened?

Jehovah

What do his words or actions reveal?

Question

What do I not yet understand?

Practice passage: Romans 8:5-9.

3. Semantic Highlighting Guide

Highlighting is a visual language. The question is not merely “What is important?” but “Why did I mark this?”

Yellow — JEHOVAHWhat does this teach me about Jehovah?
Blue — WARNING / DANGERWhat should I recognize or avoid?
Pink — SPIRITUAL COURSEWhat should I pursue or become?
Green — BLESSING / RESULTWhat good result follows Jehovah's way?
Orange — ACTIONWhat should I do?
Purple — PERSONALWhy does this matter especially to me?
  1. Read the passage before highlighting.
  2. Highlight the smallest meaningful phrase.
  3. Use color to show relationships, not decoration.
  4. Look for contrast, cause → effect, command → result, warning → consequence.
  5. Do not force every verse into a color category.
  6. If you cannot explain why you used the color, reconsider the highlight.
Practice: In Romans 8:5-9, visually map flesh versus spirit, their different outcomes, and the relationship to pleasing God.
4. Investigation Guide
NWT→Context→Cross-references→Study notes / footnotes→Research Guide / Insight / JW Library / WOL→Return to NWT

Permanent question: What did this ADD to my understanding?

For a 15-minute session, one or two useful research leads are normally enough.

5. Meditation Guide

About Jehovah

  • What does this teach me about Jehovah?
  • Why does Jehovah think or act this way?
  • What quality do I see?
  • Why is Jehovah's way better?
  • Why might Jehovah want me to know this?

About Me

  • Where is my thinking compared with Jehovah's thinking?
  • Does this reveal something I need to adjust?
  • What would I do in this situation?
  • What am I allowing to occupy my mind?
  • What would change if I believed this more deeply?

About Real Life

  • Where could I face this today?
  • What happens if I follow this counsel?
  • What could happen if I ignore it?
  • What would applying this actually look like?

Meditation passages: Psalm 63:6; Psalm 77:11, 12; Psalm 119:15; Psalm 143:5; 1 Timothy 4:15.
